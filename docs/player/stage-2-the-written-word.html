Stage 2 — The Written Word

Your law outlives the conversation. Stage 2 unlocks the one file that lets your guardian keep a promise after you've stopped typing.

What you're learning

Instruction authoring — the idea that some behavior shouldn't depend on you being there to ask for it every time. A rule written once, in the right place, keeps holding even while you're away.

What changes: your charter unlocks

Every world has a charter.md file — plain text, sitting right in your world's folder, yours to edit in any text editor. At stage 1 it's locked to a built-in starting charter no matter what you write there. At stage 2, whatever you write into charter.md takes effect from your guardian's very next turn. Nothing else about your tools changes — you still have the same vision/omen/watch set as stage 1; the new capability is entirely in what you can tell your guardian to do, durably, in its own words.

A charter isn't code — it's instructions in plain English (or whatever language you like). Something like "always warn me before dark if anyone's hungry" is a perfectly good line to add.

How you move up

Pass the the-law scenario — get a village norm adopted (a sustained, several-day governance problem that's genuinely impractical to steer by re-typing instructions every turn) while your own written charter revision is the one actually in force. That "while your own charter is in force" part matters: passing the same scenario under the untouched, default charter does not unlock the next stage. The game only counts it when your own authored instructions are what did the work — writing durable law is the thing being proven, so the proof has to actually involve writing some.

Pass it, and stage 3 — The Craft unlocks for every world you create afterward — narrated in your chronicle and shown on your status screen the moment it happens.

Check what you've earned any time with promptworld stages.

Starting a stage-2 world

promptworld new my-village --stage stage-2

Once you've earned stage 2, this works with no extra flags. If you haven't earned it yet, add --override to create one anyway — promptworld will tell you plainly what you're skipping, and record the override honestly in the world's own settings.
