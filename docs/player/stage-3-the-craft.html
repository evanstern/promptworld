Stage 3 — The Craft

You shape what it can do. Stage 3 is where you stop just instructing your guardian and start designing its actual capabilities.

What you're learning

Capability design — the idea that what an agent can do is itself something you author, not a fixed menu you're stuck with. Up through stage 2 you were writing instructions for a fixed toolset. At stage 3, the toolset itself becomes something you can extend and shape.

What unlocks

  • Skill files — extra instruction files (beyond the one main charter) that compose together, letting you build up your guardian's know-how in pieces instead of one giant file.
  • The full tool manifest — the complete non-capstone toolset opens up, and you can grant (or withhold) specific tools for a given world through its capability settings. What you don't grant is genuinely absent, not just discouraged — if a tool isn't granted, your guardian can't reach for it by accident or under pressure.

Everything from stage 1 and 2 keeps working exactly as before — this stage adds on top, it never takes anything away.

How you move up

Pass a stage-3 scenario in which a tool you personally granted — something beyond the stage's basic default — actually contributes to the pass. In other words: design a capability, then have your guardian actually use it to get the job done. That's the proof capability design is a skill you've picked up, not just a setting you toggled.

Pass it, and stage 4 — The Stewardship — the full roster, including the game's capstone capabilities — unlocks for every world you create afterward, narrated in your chronicle and shown on your status screen.

Check what you've earned any time with promptworld stages.

Starting a stage-3 world

promptworld new my-village --stage stage-3

Haven't earned it yet? Add --override and promptworld will tell you plainly what you're skipping, then create the world anyway — honestly recorded as an overridden run in the world's own settings, so it's comparable as one later.
