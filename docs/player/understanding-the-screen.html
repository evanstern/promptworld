What do all those things on screen mean?

A region-by-region tour of promptworld ui — the header, the map, the story feed, and the tabbed panel beside it — so nothing on screen stays a mystery symbol.

You don't need to memorize this page

Everything described below stays on screen the whole time you're playing — nothing here is timed or hidden. Most players pick up the handful of glyphs that show up constantly (a villager, fire, water) within their first few minutes just by watching, and only come back to look up something less common, like a rare badge or a wall glyph, when it actually appears. Keep this page open in another window if it helps; there's no penalty for checking.

The header: the top line

The very top of the screen always shows the world's current speed. Two things can join it:

  • If you've got AI-driven villagers and asked for a speed the world can't quite keep sensible thinking up with, the speed gains a short plain-language suffix, for example asked 32x — 3 minds in flight, debt 140% — the world has quietly dropped a notch on its own for now. See the time & speed page for what that means and why it happens.
  • A red [llm: provider kind] badge appears whenever a configured AI model is having trouble — unreachable, misconfigured, or answering but not actually cooperating with the game. It clears itself the moment the problem is fixed. See AI setup basics.
  • A [suppressed: class, class] badge appears whenever some kind of villager thinking (planning, conversations, meetings) is currently being skipped to keep the game running smoothly at your chosen speed. Also covered on the time & speed page.

Neither badge means anything is broken — both are the world being upfront about a trade-off it's making on your behalf.

The map

The map is a camera window that follows your villagers around as they move; arrow keys pan it manually, and c recenters on the group. As your village grows, what shows up on the map grows with it — new buildings and paths appear as villagers build them. Every symbol on the map falls into one of three families.

The feed: chronicle or raw events

Below (or beside) the map sits your village's story, in one of two forms:

  • Chronicle (the default) — a few narrated sentences per game day, written by an AI narrator, catching you up without a blow-by-blow transcript. See the reading the story page for the full picture.
  • Raw feed (press r to switch) — every single event as its own short line, in the shape TICK HH:MM type summary. Four especially important event types — a death, a gru attack, a chest theft, a broken village law — render as a whole highlighted line so they never slip by unnoticed in a busy feed.

Pause the world (space bar) and the feed becomes selectable: move through entries and a detail panel at the bottom shows that entry's full record. The keys reference card has the exact keys for selecting and scrolling.

The dock: three tabs beside the map

Keys 2/3/4 switch between three tabs; the same key again makes that tab fill the whole screen, and again (or 1) returns to the split view.

Chronicle tab

The story feed described above.

Metatron tab

Your conversation transcript with the angel, plus at a glance: your charge bank, any standing orders you've placed, a small table of every configured AI provider (model, whether it's currently up, how busy it is, and what it's cost you), and — when relevant — a line per kind of villager thinking telling you live whether it's keeping up or being skipped for speed. See playing via Metatron and the AI behind the village for what all of that actually means.

Villagers tab

A roster list — one line per villager: a selection cursor, their status, current goal, need gauges, how loaded down they are, and what they're carrying. Press ⏎ on someone to open their full detail (vitals, objective, inventory, beliefs, memories); from there, one more key opens a look at their recent decisions — what they were reacting to, what they tried, and how it turned out. The AI behind the village page covers what's actually behind those decisions.

For the exact key for every one of these actions, see the keys reference card — this page is about what you're looking at, that one is purely about which key does what.
