What do all those things on screen mean?

A region-by-region tour of promptworld ui — the header, the map, the story feed, and the tabbed panel beside it — so nothing on screen stays a mystery symbol.

You don't need to memorize this page

Everything described below stays on screen the whole time you're playing — nothing here is timed or hidden. Most players pick up the handful of glyphs that show up constantly (a villager, fire, water) within their first few minutes just by watching, and only come back to look up something less common, like a rare badge or a wall glyph, when it actually appears. Keep this page open in another window if it helps; there's no penalty for checking.

Or just press ? — this tour is built in

Press ? at almost any moment in the game and a help overlay opens right there: the keys that work on the screen you're on, a walkthrough of the same regions this page covers — the header and every badge it can show, the full map glyph table, and the tabs — every stage-unlock ceremony you've ever earned in this world (in case you missed or dismissed one), and a page on the guardian itself: your stage, what it teaches, every verb it grants, and one example ask per verb. Press tab inside it to move between sections, and ? or esc to close it and land exactly where you were — it never pauses or changes anything in the world. It works identically in a village with no AI configured, so help is always there even when the guardian isn't. The one place ? doesn't open it is while you're typing a message to the guardian (it types a question mark, as you'd want).

The header: the top line

The very top of the screen always shows the world's current speed. A few things can join — or replace — it:

  • If you've got AI-driven villagers and asked for a speed the world can't quite keep sensible thinking up with, the speed gains a short plain-language suffix, for example asked 32x — 3 minds in flight, debt 140% — the world has quietly dropped a notch on its own for now. See the time & speed page for what that means and why it happens.
  • A red [llm: provider kind] badge appears whenever a configured AI model is having trouble — unreachable, misconfigured, or answering but not actually cooperating with the game. It clears itself the moment the problem is fixed. See AI setup basics.
  • A [suppressed: class, class] badge appears whenever some kind of villager thinking (planning, conversations, meetings) is currently being skipped to keep the game running smoothly at your chosen speed. Also covered on the time & speed page.
  • A small dim [lesson] badge appears once your world has grown past the early learning stages (or isn't using the stage ladder at all). It's a quiet reminder that the game's lesson tips live in the ? help overlay's lessons section rather than popping up on screen — see the lesson bar below.
  • If your run has ended — the last villager has died — the usual running/PAUSED state gives way to a bold red ENDED marker. The world is now a read-only archive: the pause and speed keys do nothing (the footer hint changes to say run ended (read-only)), while everything you read — the map, the story, the villagers, the morgue — keeps working. The reading the story page covers what an ended world leaves you to read.

None of these means anything is broken — each is the world being upfront about its own state on your behalf.

The villager strip: your whole roster, at a glance

One plain line sits right under the header, always on: a count of your villagers plus one letter per villager, in the same style the map uses — bright for awake, dim for asleep, faint † for dead. Clicking a letter jumps the map camera straight to that villager (a dead villager's grave counts); the keyboard way to do the same jump is J on the villagers tab, which lists everyone in the same order. Beyond that one shortcut it's a glanceability layer — everything it shows is also in the villagers tab in full. If your terminal gets too short to fit it, it folds into a quiet [N villagers] badge in the header instead of disappearing; it never appears at all in the narrow single-pane layout.

The map

The map is a camera window that follows your villagers around as they move; arrow keys pan it manually, and c recenters on the group. As your village grows, what shows up on the map grows with it — new buildings and paths appear as villagers build them. Every symbol on the map falls into one of three families.

A living villager's letter can also change style (never its letter or case) to flag trouble: bold red underline means one of their needs has fallen into real danger — including the moment the game calls a villager out for neglecting a need (that alert arrives as a highlighted line in the story feed; on the map the same red underline already covers it) — and a cooler, faint tint means their last thought got skipped to keep the world running at speed (see the time & speed page). If both are true at once, the danger styling wins — a body in trouble always outranks a skipped thought.

Looking at a specific tile

Press v and a highlighted cursor appears on the map — move it with the same keys that pan the map, or click any visible tile with the mouse — and the tabbed panel beside the map temporarily shows a tile details view instead of whichever tab you had open: everything on that one tile, top to bottom — any villager standing there (with their needs and current goal), any pile or chest and its contents, any building and its state (a fire's lit/dying/cold, a wall's damage), the ground itself in plain words, and any recent event recorded there. A short header also names how warm and how bright the tile is right now, and — where it matters — whether that keeps a villager safe from the gru at night. This is a temporary loan of the panel, not a new tab: whatever tab you actually had open — the story feed mid-scroll, a villager's detail view — is exactly where you left it the moment you turn the cursor off (v or esc) or switch tabs directly with 2-5. Press ⏎ or tab to move into the tile's list (its border glows to show it has your attention) and select a row for a full look — a villager's whole profile, or an event's raw record. See the keys reference card for the exact keys and mouse clicks.

The feed: chronicle or raw events

Below (or beside) the map sits your village's story, in one of two forms:

  • Chronicle (the default) — a few narrated sentences per game day, written by an AI narrator, catching you up without a blow-by-blow transcript. See the reading the story page for the full picture.
  • Raw feed (press r to switch) — every single event as its own short line, in the shape TICK HH:MM type summary. A handful of especially important event types — a death, a gru attack, a theft (by a villager or by the night stranger), a broken village law, and a villager the game has caught neglecting a vital need for hours without doing anything about it — render as a whole highlighted line so they never slip by unnoticed in a busy feed.

Pause the world (space bar) and the feed becomes selectable: move through entries and a detail panel at the bottom shows that entry's full record. That panel's bottom row is an actions bar: for most entries it offers to jump the map camera to where the event happened — press ⏎, or click the entry's line with the mouse, the game's first mouse-clickable control — and for entries with no map location (a conversation, say) it plainly says "no location for this event" instead of pretending there's somewhere to jump. The keys reference card has the exact keys for selecting and scrolling.

The dock: four tabs beside the map (five on a scenario world)

Keys 2/3/4/5 switch between four tabs; the same key again makes that tab fill the whole screen, and again (or 1) returns to the split view.

Chronicle tab

The story feed described above.

Guardian tab

Your conversation transcript with the guardian (the village's watcher, previously called Metatron), plus at a glance: your charge bank, your world's stage if it's on the learning ladder (at the first stage this also notes that the guardian's charter is locked to its built-in preset), and any standing orders you've placed. This tab is purely the conversation and orders now — the technical dashboards that used to crowd it live on their own tab, next. See playing via the guardian for what all of that actually means.

Villagers tab

A roster list — one line per villager: a selection cursor, their status, current goal, need gauges, how loaded down they are, and what they're carrying. Press ⏎ on someone to open their full detail (vitals, objective, inventory, beliefs, memories); from there, one more key opens a look at their recent decisions — what they were reacting to, what they tried, and how it turned out. The AI behind the village page covers what's actually behind those decisions.

Systems tab

The technical dashboards, moved here out of the guardian tab's way: a small table of every configured AI provider (model, whether it's currently up, how busy it is, and what it's cost you so far this month), followed by a "cognition horizon" block — one line per kind of villager thinking (planning, conversations, meetings) telling you live whether it's keeping up or being skipped for speed, with a running skipped-thought count. A world with no AI configured at all says so plainly here instead of showing an empty table. See the AI behind the village for what all of this actually means.

Exercise tab (scenario worlds only)

A world created to run a specific practice exercise (see the stage pages) gains a fifth tab, key 6, alongside the other four — an ordinary world simply doesn't have it. The first time you open it, a short briefing explains what the exercise is asking for; press any key to move past it. After that, it shows one row per thing the exercise is watching for (a checkmark once it's satisfied, otherwise a "pending" marker), an optional list of pressures the world will apply and roughly when, and — once the run either passes or ends — a plain pass/fail banner naming the outcome.

The guardian console: the conversation, full screen

Press G (capital) from the split view, a solo tab, or the narrow layout to open the guardian console — a dedicated full-screen page for your conversation with the guardian, laid out as a readable document rather than the guardian tab's compact scroll: each turn, yours and the guardian's alike, gets its own labeled block. Beside the conversation sits a charter/skills panel showing exactly what's binding right now — whether your charter is the default, your own writing, or locked to a stage preset, and whether any skill files are bound or still locked. Press e to open charter.md in your own text editor (whatever your $EDITOR is set to) directly from the console — there's no in-game text editor, just a handoff to your own — and the moment you save and return, the console confirms "charter changed — next turn binds it." J and K scroll back through earlier turns. G, 1, or esc all return you to whatever view you had open before. On a scenario world, or once your guardian has written itself a report card (see playing via the guardian), the console also shows a card near the top: a rubric checklist first, if one exists, then the guardian's own attribution note beneath it. See playing via the guardian for what the charter actually does, and the keys reference card for every key the console responds to.

When a run ends, or a stage unlocks

Two moments take over the whole screen, keyboard and all, until you dismiss them: earning a new stage opens a short ceremony — the narrated moment plus the report card that earned it — and your last villager dying opens a full-screen postmortem — how the run ended, the report card if the world was scored, and the morgue's evidence rows for every death. The report card tells the truth: each goal is graded for real, so a goal the run failed shows a ✗ with the honest count behind it (two deaths on a "no villager dies" goal is a ✗, never a checkmark), while a card backed by an exercise you already passed shows every goal met — it re-reads the pass you earned rather than re-judging you. Press esc to dismiss either one, or q to quit/detach. Missed or dismissed a ceremony? The ? help overlay keeps a permanent replay of every one you've ever earned in that world. See the keys reference card for the exact keys.

The lesson bar: the game teaching itself, once per lesson

In a young world (learning stages 1 and 2), the first time something new happens — the first time a thought is skipped for speed, the first gru attack, the first time one of your standing orders expires, the first death, the first time the guardian refuses an action — a two-line tip appears just above the guardian strip: one sentence saying what just happened, and an arrow line pointing at the key or tab that shows you more, always ending (? for more · x dismiss).

Three things keep it polite: only one tip shows at a time, each lesson appears once ever (across all your worlds — the game remembers what it has already taught you), and a tip stays up until you either do the thing it points at or press x. Anything you dismiss remains readable forever in the ? overlay's lessons section. Past the early stages the bar stays out of your way entirely — just the [lesson] header badge above, with everything in the overlay.

The guardian strip: charge status, always in view

Above the box where you type to the guardian sits one plain line with no border, visible no matter which of the three tabs is open: your charge bank as a row of lightning-bolt glyphs plus a (used/cap) count, when your next charge arrives (next +1 @ <time>) if the bank isn't already full and a recharge is actually coming (the pace follows the village's faith, and at rock-bottom faith in a scenario world no recharge is scheduled at all — so none is forecast), how many standing orders you currently have running, and the village's faith as a plain faith N number. Anything that doesn't apply yet — no snapshot loaded yet, or a bank that's already full — simply doesn't show, rather than displaying a misleading zero; against an older game daemon that doesn't report faith yet, the segment reads faith — instead of inventing a number.

If your terminal window gets too short to fit everything, this strip is the very last thing to give way: instead of disappearing, its content folds into the dim placeholder text inside the empty message box instead. In a narrow terminal (below the map+dock split), it still appears in the same spot — right above the message box on the guardian's own tab.

For the exact key for every one of these actions, see the keys reference card — this page is about what you're looking at, that one is purely about which key does what.
