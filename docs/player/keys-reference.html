Keys reference

Every key promptworld ui responds to, grouped by what's on screen when you press it. Pure controls — for what any of this is actually showing you, see what do all those things on screen mean instead.

One thing to know up front: the whole game is playable from the keyboard alone — every key below reaches everything, and that stays true no matter what. Mouse support is a separate, additive convenience layered on top; today it covers three things — the chronicle jump described below, clicking a map tile (described in "Looking at a specific tile"), and clicking a row in that tile's detail list — and everywhere else a click simply does nothing.

The guardian console (after pressing G)

A full-screen page for the conversation — each turn rendered as its own labeled block, a charter/skills panel beside it, and a hook into your own text editor. Most other keys (space, [ / ], 2-5, pan, a / t, r, q) still work exactly as in the normal view underneath.

KeyAction
G / 1 / escClose, back to whatever was open before
mStart typing to the guardian (the same message box as everywhere else)
eOpen charter.md in your own text editor (your $EDITOR); on return, the console confirms "charter changed — next turn binds it"
J / KScroll toward the latest turn / scroll back toward older ones

Looking back through the feed (pause the world first)

All of the normal-view keys above still work here too — these are additions, not replacements.

KeyAction
j / kSelect the next / previous entry
g / GJump to the first / last entry
J / KScroll a long entry's detail
⏎, or click the entry's lineJump the map camera to where that event happened — same clamped pan c uses to recenter. If the event has no place (a conversation, say), the detail pane says "no location for this event" instead and nothing moves
spaceResume (also clears your selection)

Clicking an entry's line is the game's first mouse control — everywhere else, a click still does nothing.

Looking at a specific tile (press v)

Press v from the normal view and a highlighted cursor appears on the map — move it anywhere, and a details panel takes over the tab area on the right, listing everything on that one tile: any villager standing there (with their needs and what they're doing), any pile or chest, any building, the ground itself, and recent goings-on there. Press v again (or esc) to put that panel back to whatever it was showing before — nothing you had open is lost.

KeyAction
vToggle the tile cursor on/off
h j k l or the arrow keys, or click a tileMove the cursor one tile (a click also turns the cursor on if it wasn't already)
H J K LJump the cursor 8 tiles at once, stopping at the edge of the map
cSnap the view to follow the cursor
⏎ or tabMove into the details panel (its border glows to show it has your attention)
2-5 (6 on a scenario world) Turn the cursor off and switch straight to that tab
escStep back one level at a time — out of a detail you drilled into, then out of the details panel, then off the cursor entirely

Once you've moved into the details panel (⏎/tab), or clicked one of its rows:

KeyAction
j / k, or click a rowSelect a row in the list
⏎, or click the already-selected row againOpen that row's full detail — a villager's full profile, or the raw record of a past event
esc or tabBack out one level

Every tile also shows how warm and how bright it is right now, in plain words (e.g. "warm — in a lit fire's radius", "dark — indoors"), and — where relevant — a note on whether that keeps a villager safe from the gru.

When the run ends

Two full-screen moments take over the keyboard entirely — while either is on screen, every other key does nothing (except ctrl+c, which always quits).

A stage-unlock ceremony appears the moment you earn a new stage: a short celebration naming what you just proved and what it unlocks.

The postmortem appears once, the moment a run ends (your last villager has died) — a full-screen summary of how the run went. You can also reopen it any time afterward with p from the normal view.

KeyCeremonyPostmortem
escDismiss (returns to whatever was beneath — nothing else is force-closed)Dismiss — reopen later with p
qQuit/detach — the world keeps running Quit/detach — plainly, since the run itself has actually ended
everything else, including ?Does nothing — the takeover keeps the screen until you press esc or q

Missed or dismissed a ceremony? Nothing is lost — the help overlay's ceremonies section (below) keeps a permanent replay of every stage you've ever unlocked in that world.

The help overlay (after pressing ?)

The overlay shows this same information in-game — the keys for whatever screen you were just on, a tour of everything on screen, every lesson the game can teach you (including ones you dismissed with x — nothing is ever lost), a permanent replay of every stage-unlock ceremony you've earned in this world, and a page on the guardian itself: your current stage, what it teaches, every verb it grants you, one example ask per verb, and — below that — the whole ladder ahead of you: every stage in order, what each one teaches, what earns it, and whether you've already earned it (with a note on where, if you have). It never touches the world: opening it doesn't pause anything, and closing it puts you back exactly where you were. One more courtesy: if a warning badge is showing in the header when you press ? (degraded speed, an AI provider problem, a skipped-thinking notice), the overlay opens straight to the "tour of everything on screen" page, scrolled right to the line that explains that badge — you don't have to go hunting for it.

KeyAction
? or escClose the overlay
tab / shift+tabCycle its five sections: keys → the screen → lessons → ceremonies → the guardian
tKeys section: switch between the basic and advanced key lists
n / pKeys section: page through every mode's key list — normal view, typing to the guardian, looking back through the feed, the villagers tab (roster and detail), the split/single view, and (new) looking at a specific tile
J / KScroll a long page

The guardian section always shows something useful, even on a brand-new world or with no AI configured at all — the current-stage part reads only your world's stage, never anything the guardian says, and the ladder below it reads your own earned-stages record (the same one promptworld stages reads from a terminal) — never anything from this specific world, so it looks the same no matter which world you're in.
