# Quickstart Guide Patterns

How complex sim games structure the "sit down and play your first session" document — the
genre's answer to "how do you play this game?". The canonical example is the
[Dwarf Fortress Quickstart guide](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Quickstart_guide); Cataclysm:
DDA's [Getting started](https://cataclysmdda.miraheze.org/wiki/Getting_started) follows the
same shape.

## Recurring structural elements

Observed section order in the DF Quickstart (see [[_grounding]] § Dwarf Fortress):

1. **UI/notation primer first** — before any gameplay: how keys are written ("t means press
   t without shift; T means shift+t"), how menus navigate, what interface symbols mean.
2. **Setup steps** (world generation, embark) as a literal do-this-then-this sequence.
3. **A "minimal viable session"** — the DF guide's core is titled *A Minimal Fortress*: the
   smallest set of actions that produces a functioning game state (entry, storage, food,
   workshops). Nothing optional appears here.
4. **"Beyond minimal"** — the same systems, one level deeper, clearly marked as optional.
5. **"What next?"** — explicit hand-off to the deeper reference corpus (the wiki).

## Tone devices that recur

- **Scope statement in the first line**: "for those who have never played before and quickly
  want to jump in head-first" — the guide says who it is for ([[_grounding]]).
- **Failure reassurance up front**: "Always remember that losing is fun!… losing means that
  next time, *you'll remember how you lost*" — the guide sets the expectation that the first
  sessions will fail, before instructions begin ([[_grounding]]).
- **Naming the reader's confusion**: "you have no idea what to do… That's understandable" —
  the reader's disorientation is addressed directly rather than ignored ([[_grounding]]).
- **Why alongside how**: each instruction carries a one-clause rationale (refuse stays
  outside "to avoid miasma"), so steps are memorable rather than ritual ([[_grounding]]).
- **Plain-terms definitions at first mention**: a stockpile is "where your dwarves will drop
  things for storage" — jargon is defined in the sentence that introduces it ([[_grounding]]).

## Audience splitting

The DF wiki maintains **separate quickstarts per mode/audience** (fortress mode, adventure
mode, military) instead of one long guide ([[_grounding]]). CDDA similarly tiers its docs:
one Getting-started walkthrough plus a graded ladder of community guides "from basic
tutorials to advanced" ([[_grounding]] § Cataclysm).
