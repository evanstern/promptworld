# Healthy Engagement vs Dark Patterns

What published work says distinguishes exploitative retention from healthy engagement
([[_grounding]] § Area 3). The corpus splits into a dark-patterns taxonomy, an
intrinsic-motivation literature, and session-respecting design research.

## The dark-patterns side

- Founding definition (Zagal, Björk & Lewis, FDG 2013): patterns "used intentionally by a
  game creator to cause negative experiences for players which are against their best
  interests and likely to happen without their consent." Categories: **temporal**
  ("playing by appointment," "grinding"), **monetary** ("pay to skip"), **social**
  ([FDG 2013 PDF](http://www.fdg2013.org/program/papers/paper06_zagal_etal.pdf)).
- CHI 2022 extends the frame to mobile games exploiting "temporal, monetary, social, and
  psychological means" ([[_grounding]]).
- SDT-based analysis: deceptive patterns are profitable designs that frustrate "autonomy,
  competence, and relatedness"; streaks specifically "pressure users into constant
  engagement to avoid losing progress, which can create anxiety and compulsive behavior";
  FOMO is "extrinsic coercion" that drives engagement toward extrinsic rewards, possibly
  hindering "the intrinsic reward of pure enjoyment" ([[_grounding]] § dark patterns).
- Ethical-alternative frameworks ("Radiant Patterns") are proposed but described as "mainly
  theoretical"; a dissenting paper ("Against 'Dark Game Design Patterns'") disputes the
  concept itself — the taxonomy is debated, not settled ([[_grounding]]).

## The intrinsic-motivation side

- Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006): satisfaction of autonomy, competence, and relatedness
  "independently predict enjoyment and future game play" — the basis of the PENS measure,
  which treats "basic need satisfaction [as] the pathway to enjoyable and engaging game
  experiences" ([paper PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf),
  [[_grounding]] § SDT).
- Overjustification caution: adding external rewards (points, badges, streaks) to
  already-enjoyed activities "often decreases" intrinsic motivation ([[_grounding]]).

## Session-respecting design

- "A natural stopping point is a down period after a major moment in action" (save room,
  chapter end); players who felt a session was *complete* when play ended "felt less
  frustrated than those who were cut out in the middle of a quest" ([[_grounding]] § natural
  stopping points; [UCT thesis](https://open.uct.ac.za/handle/11427/29558)).
- The research calls for games "that have natural end points" and make quitting at one's
  own volition easy; industry advice mirrors it: first sessions need "a natural end point
  where players are in the best possible state to return" ([[_grounding]]).
- Developer practice on temporal design (daily quests, seasons, session metrics) is
  documented in a CHI 2026 interview study ([[_grounding]]).

## The distinction the sources draw

Across the corpus the line is drawn consistently: dark patterns retain by **loss-framing
and coercion** (streak punishment, appointments, FOMO), while healthy engagement retains by
**need satisfaction** (competence growth, autonomy, relatedness) plus **session shape**
(natural stopping points, respect for the player's own rhythm). Zachtronics' no-reward
optimization histograms are recorded elsewhere in this branch as a concrete instance of the
intrinsic pole ([[Puzzle-Pedagogy-Patterns]]).
