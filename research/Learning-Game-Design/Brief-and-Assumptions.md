# Brief and Assumptions

## The request (restated)

TASK-120: research **learning-game design proper** — the gap jointly flagged by the two
sibling research passes done 2026-07-24 (one on gameplay patterns of colony sims /
roguelikes / god games, one on player-docs conventions of TUI games), both of which had
zero sources on how games *teach real skills* by design. Six coverage areas were named:

1. Puzzle-as-programming-pedagogy (Zachtronics family and adjacent).
2. Tutorial / onboarding-curve literature (Portal, George Fan's rules, flow theory).
3. Healthy retention design — "addictive in a good way" (dark patterns vs. intrinsic
   motivation, session-respecting design).
4. Roguelike meta-progression and the skill-dilution debate.
5. RimWorld learning-helper **per-lesson anatomy** (deeper than "it exists").
6. How observe-mostly games explain the observe/intervene split to new players.

## Constraints carried into the research

- **In scope:** cited facts about how existing games and published research handle
  teaching-through-play, onboarding curves, ethical engagement, progress-across-failure,
  adaptive lesson systems, and observation-first onboarding.
- **Out of scope:** any recommendation for promptworld. Verdicts, comparisons-with-intent,
  and "should" statements are the analysis phase's job, not this branch's.

## Assumptions

- "A game such as this" = promptworld: an LLM prompting → agents-and-tools **learning
  game** — a TUI ambient sim of an LLM-agent village where the player authors prompts and
  charters for an angel intermediary (Metatron), and the village acts as the eval harness.
  (Context duplicated here per branch isolation; this branch links to no other branch.)
- That shape weights the research toward: (a) games that teach programming-shaped skills
  through play rather than instruction, (b) games whose player mostly observes and
  intervenes sparsely, (c) run/failure structures where progress must survive bad outcomes,
  and (d) engagement mechanics that retain without exploiting.
- Community debate threads (ResetEra, Steam discussions) are recorded as evidence *that a
  debate exists and what its positions are*, not as authority on design fact.

## Known thin spots (recorded honestly)

- God-game onboarding: no first-party writing found on teaching the observe/intervene
  split; the WorldBox evidence is players reporting the tutorial was hard to find.
- Rogue Legacy: no first-party (Cellar Door) design source surfaced; its role in the
  corpus rests on secondary genre coverage.
- The CHI 2022 "A Game of Dark Patterns" paper and the RimWorld wiki page were both
  paywalled/403 to direct fetch; claims from them are quoted via their public abstracts
  and search excerpts.
