# Observation-Driven Play

What the sources record about games where the player's primary verb is *watching* a
simulation run — the least command-driven end of the spectrum.

## The idle framing: set conditions, watch complexity unfold

The idle-game pattern: "you set up initial conditions for a simulation and watch the
resulting complexity unfold. You can interact with the simulation sometimes as it runs, but
most of the time you just enjoy watching it play out" ([[_grounding]] § Observation-driven).

**Progress Quest** is the limit case — after character creation "the rest of the experience
happens on its own… your character does quests on their own, defeats monsters on their own
and chooses their own upgrades." Its noted staying power: "you accrue progress over time and
**events indicate a narrative about the digital person running on your machine**"
([[_grounding]]) — an event feed about an autonomous character is itself the retention hook.

## Generative Agents (Smallville): the player inside an LLM-agent sim

The Stanford Generative Agents work is the direct research precedent for an LLM-agent
village and documents what the human's role can be ([[_grounding]] § Generative Agents):

- 25 agents in a Sims-like town, each seeded only with "a paragraph description of their
  personality and motivations"; agents store experiences as natural-language memories,
  "synthesize those memories over time into higher-level reflections, and retrieve them
  dynamically to plan behavior."
- **Interaction is natural language, in persona**: "end users interact with the sandbox
  environment through natural language," taking on personas to converse with agents.
- **One seeded intention cascades**: from "a single user-specified notion that one agent
  wants to throw a Valentine's Day party, the agents autonomously spread invitations…, ask
  each other out on dates to the party, and coordinate to show up… at the right time" —
  information diffusion, relationship formation, and temporal coordination emerging from a
  single nudge. The player's minimal act (planting one idea) produces multi-day observable
  consequences.
