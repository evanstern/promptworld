# Emergent Narrative and Losing Is Fun

How Dwarf Fortress and the roguelike tradition turn failure into the product rather than
the punishment.

## No win condition → every story ends, and that's the story

DF has "no predefined goal, endpoint, or win condition"; because of that, "every playthrough
will eventually end in disaster… part of the fun is also discovering how your lovingly
crafted civilization meets its doom" ([[_grounding]] § Dwarf Fortress). The community motto
"losing is fun!" encodes learning-through-failure as the intended play pattern
([[_grounding]]).

## Emergent narrative is retold, not rendered

The academic definition observed for DF: emergent narrative is "stories imagined and
possibly retold by players recounting their experiences in a game, often adding details not
present in the game itself" ([[_grounding]]). The canonical artifact — **Boatmurdered** — is
a player-written succession chronicle, "credited for introducing both Dwarf Fortress and the
Let's Play format to a broader audience" ([[_grounding]]). The game supplies uncompromising
simulation logic; the *story object* is produced by players narrating what happened.

## Permadeath: irreversibility, not pain

- Rogue's co-creator Glenn Wichman: what matters to the genre "is simply the inability to
  undo decisions that lead to death, rather than permanently stopping someone's playthrough"
  — permadeath was "never supposed to be about pain" ([[_grounding]] § Roguelike permadeath).
- Consequences that can't be undone drive both attachment and story: irreversibility
  "increases the feeling of responsibility for your actions… The player is more attached to
  their character, for fear of losing them," and each death opens "a new narrative arc"
  instead of a repeat, since a roguelike regenerates its content each run ([[_grounding]]).

## Attachment follows the failure model

The DF-vs-RimWorld comparison records opposite attachment behaviors: RimWorld's scripted
pressure leads one author to deliberately avoid attachment to settlers ("they're not gonna
be around for too long"), while DF players nickname survivors of harrowing events and
engrave memorial slabs ([[_grounding]] § The DF vs RimWorld contrast). See
[[Simulation-vs-Director]] for the underlying design split.
