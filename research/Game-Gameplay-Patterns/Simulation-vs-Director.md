# Simulation vs Director

The corpus documents two opposite engines for producing stories in a colony sim, laid out
most sharply in the Game Developer comparison of Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld
([[_grounding]] § The DF vs RimWorld contrast).

## The two poles

| | Dwarf Fortress (simulation-driven) | RimWorld (director-driven) |
|---|---|---|
| Events come from | the simulation's own systems interacting | a director AI reacting to player state |
| Reacts to | "its own systems, not to player pacing" | "player progress, shaping events to keep the story engaging" |
| Optimizes for | "unfettered simulation without concern for user experience" | "streamlined gameplay and player control… intentional pacing" |
| Arc guarantee | none — drama is unscheduled | pressure/release cycles by design |

Direct quotes in [[_grounding]]. The much-quoted summary of the DF pole: "Dwarf Fortress
does not strive to be a good video game… It's a good video game seemingly by coincidence."

## Consequences recorded in the sources

- **Predictability**: Cassandra's cooldown windows make RimWorld's rhythm legible;
  DF's drama arrives whenever the simulation produces it ([[_grounding]] §§ RimWorld,
  DF-vs-RimWorld). Randy Random exists as RimWorld's opt-in to the unscheduled pole
  ([[Storyteller-Driven-Pacing]]).
- **Attachment**: the RimWorld player quoted avoids bonding with settlers under scheduled
  pressure; the DF player memorializes individual dwarves ([[_grounding]];
  [[Emergent-Narrative-and-Losing-Is-Fun]]).
- These are poles, not a binary: RimWorld still runs a deep simulation underneath its
  director, and DF's Steam release added pacing conveniences — the sources describe design
  centers of gravity ([[_grounding]]).
