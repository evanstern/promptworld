# Indirect Control and Divine Intervention

The god-game genre (Populous → Black & White → WorldBox) is the established template for
play through a divine intermediary — the closest genre precedent for a player who acts on a
simulated population without commanding it ([[_grounding]] § God games).

## The defining constraint: no direct unit control

Genre definition from the sources: "the player does not move individual units like in an
RTS, but instead influences the environment or general directives, and the simulation
responds"; god games are "a subgenre of artificial life game, where players use supernatural
powers to indirectly influence a population of simulated worshipers" ([[_grounding]]). Play
is a dialogue with a simulation, not command of it.

## The intervention vocabulary

Recorded intervention types: "causing natural disasters, blessing crops, or answering
prayers"; manifestations are "miracles, disasters, or environmental changes," plus
terraforming ([[_grounding]]). The verbs are all *world-level* — the player touches
conditions and events, and the simulated people respond by their own logic.

## The mana economy: intervention is budgeted and endogenous

The genre's core loop ties intervention capacity to the simulated population's state:
"players must economize quantities of power or mana **derived from the size and prosperity
of their population of worshipers**… a positive feedback loop where more power allows the
player to help their population grow and gain more power" ([[_grounding]]). Intervention is
neither free nor external — the sim's wellbeing is the player's action budget, which is what
makes restraint and timing into gameplay.
